# TopoR: what it does, and what pcb-maker can take from it

TopoR ("Topological Router") is a commercial autorouter from Eremex (St.
Petersburg), developed since 1988 by S. Yu. Luzin and O. B. Polubasov
(LETI). It routes on a triangulation instead of a grid and draws any-angle
tracks with arcs. This review is from 2026-09-29. Its sources are:
- the TopoR 7.0 manual (2018, 315 pages);
- the authors' papers:
  - Luzin & Polubasov, ChipNews 2002 ([chipnews](http://www.chipnews.ru/html.cgi/arhiv/02_05/9.htm));
  - Polubasov, Electronics 2010 ([article 112](https://www.electronics.ru/journal/article/112));
  - Luzin, Popov & Popov 2013 ([article 3556](https://www.electronics.ru/journal/article/3556));
- the product pages;
- independent reports.

The key claims below were checked against the source text.

## The "instant 100 %" claim

- **The claim.** The English site lists "'Instant routing' of 100% of wires
  on a board" ([t.eremex.com](https://t.eremex.com/)).
- **The Russian product page qualifies it.** Routing 100 % of the nets
  happens "practically instantly, within a few seconds. All connections are
  routed, even if some technological constraints have to be violated". A
  multi-criteria optimization then removes violations, shortens wires and
  cuts vias
  ([eremex.ru](https://www.eremex.ru/products/delta-design/topor/)).
- **The manual says the same.** "Full (100 %) routing is achieved almost
  immediately, but DRC violations are possible"
  (manual, section "Automatic routing"). It adds that during optimization
  "a 0 in the Violations column does not guarantee there are no real
  violations", because vias are modelled as stretches of wire (p. 87).
- **So "instant 100 %" is a connected but illegal state.** A legal board
  comes from the optimization, which takes minutes to hours:
  - an independent 2-layer test took about 1 h of CPU ([Levine
    2017](https://wp.josh.com/2017/10/23/adventures-in-autorouting/));
  - Eremex's own testimonial reports 20 min of routing plus 40 min of
    editing.
- **pcb-maker already has this state.** Its first negotiation iteration
  routes every net with overlaps allowed, in seconds. What counts is the
  time to a *legal* board: 22 s for Interf-U, 3 s for PIC (router.md).

## The algorithm, as published

- **Topological model.** One Delaunay triangulation, shared by all layers,
  with vertices on pads, barriers and polygons.
  - A wire is only the sequence of triangulation edges it crosses, and its
    order on each edge.
  - A via is fixed only to the stretch between two crossings.
  - Wires that cross nothing have no layer yet.
- **Initial routing.** Sequential, widest nets first, then the shortest.
  - Each wire takes the shortest path on the triangulation within the
    clearance capacity, and among those the one with the fewest crossings.
  - Double crossings are removed by swapping net fragments. Functionally
    equivalent pins are used to uncross wires.
  - Layers are assigned afterwards, so that crossing wires end up on
    different layers.
- **Optimization.** Rip-up and reroute, one wire at a time.
  - The cost is a weighted sum of about 100 factors: length, vias,
    violations, crossings, and wedging between another wire's segments.
  - The weights are chosen per wire and randomly oscillated.
  - Global layer re-assignment keeps every path and never adds a via. It is
    exact on two layers, approximated one layer pair at a time on more, and
    randomized among equally good results to leave local minima.
  - An archive keeps the variants on the convex hull of (length, vias): F1
    prices vias cheaply, F2 dearly. Each variant is labelled with the via
    price range where it is best, and descents restart from random archived
    variants, possibly in parallel.
- **Geometry ("metrization").** Wires become the shortest path in their
  homotopy class: circular arcs at clearance radius around obstacles,
  joined by tangents. They are computed on a per-layer quasi-triangulation
  that handles long pads. Moving parts or vias recomputes the geometry and
  keeps the topology. How this is done is not published ("beyond the scope
  of the article", 2010).
- **Other techniques (2013).**
  - Delay equalization in parallel: reserve room, push neighbours, fill
    with zigzags.
  - BGA fanout from the centre out.
- **No patents were found.** There is only a Rospatent software
  registration (No. 2005611370).

## What it builds on

Each of these is published and summarized in the
[algorithm survey](2026-09-26-algorithm-survey/4-geometry-topology.md),
ideas 1 to 4:
- Bazilevich's "flexible routing" on a triangulated field (USSR, 1974-81),
  which the authors name as their origin;
- rubber-band sketches: Leiserson & Maley 1985; SURF (Dai, Dayan &
  Staepelaere, DAC 1991); Dayan's thesis, 1997;
- homotopic shortest paths with fat edges: Hershberger & Snoeyink 1994;
  Duncan et al. 2006;
- exact design-rule checking during topological search: Hama & Etoh, IBM,
  ASP-DAC 1997, which targets exactly the weakness TopoR admits.

Open-source relatives:
- [Topola](https://codeberg.org/topola/topola) (Rust, MIT, NLnet-funded).
  It does rubber-band routing on a Delaunay navmesh with an exact
  arc/line kernel. It has no release yet and is going through an
  architectural overhaul for performance.
- The gEDA Toporouter (C, 2008, abandoned).
- Salewski's Ruby and Nim routers (experimental).

## Features, and where pcb-maker stands

| TopoR | pcb-maker |
| --- | --- |
| Any-angle tracks with arcs and tangents; a 45° mode | 45° lattice tracks, no arcs |
| Topology-preserving part and via moves, recomputed instantly | Moves reroute the affected nets: 30-60 s per trial |
| Exact 2-layer via minimization by global layer assignment | Layers chosen during the A* by via cost; vias 0.5-0.56 times the designers' |
| Archive of length/via trade-offs | Seeds race, best of (open, vias, copper) |
| Differential pairs, delay equalization, BGA fanout, teardrops, neck-down | None of these |
| Pin and gate swap | Pin swap (opt-in; swap candidates listed) |
| Automatic placement (since 6.1) | Electrostatic placer, constraints, coupled loop |
| DRC on its own model, admittedly incomplete | KiCad's own ERC/DRC as the gate, plus an exact internal verifier |
| KiCad only through Specctra DSN/SES, where arcs arrive as segments | Reads and writes KiCad natively; KiCad has native arcs |
| Windows, $1,990-9,990; Lite limited to 125 connections | Open source |

## Can we replicate it?

Yes, the parts that matter. Everything but the geometry step is published
in enough detail, and the geometry step has published equivalents. The
question is which parts are worth it. By value per effort:

1. **Arcs and exact channels by homotopic shortest paths.** Take each
   routed net's corridor (the triangles its lattice path passes). Pull the
   net tight within that corridor, around obstacles inflated by
   clearance + half-width. The result is tangents and arcs, correct by
   construction, emitted as KiCad arcs.
   - It gives what TopoR's boards look like: shorter wires, no staircases,
     no sharp corners.
   - Done inside negotiation rather than after it, it would also fit wires
     where the lattice cannot. The LNS experiment found boards where two
     nets compete for a gap the designer fitted both through.
   - As a post-pass it needs no change to the router: the survey estimates
     2-3 days (idea 4).
2. **Topology-preserving moves in the layout loop.** With nets held as
   sketches (triangle sequences plus order), a trial part move recomputes
   geometry and checks cut capacities in milliseconds, and reroutes only
   the nets whose topology no longer fits. The move phase is the largest
   cost of `layout-kicad-board` (30-60 s per trial, up to 600 s), so this
   is the big speed-up. It needs item 1 and a sketch representation
   (survey ideas 1 and 2).
3. **Layer assignment after routing.** Route with crossings allowed, then
   choose layers as a planar max-cut (exact on two layers: Chen, Kajitani
   & Chan 1983; Barahona et al. 1988), then reroute the nets that gained
   vias. Our vias are already about half the designers'; this is worth a
   measurement, not a rewrite.
4. **Trade-off archive and randomized weights.** Keep the routings on the
   (length, vias) convex hull from seeds and moves, and let the user or
   agent pick. Cheap.
5. **A full topological router in place of the lattice.** The largest
   effort and risk: SURF took years and Topola is still reworking its core.
   Not recommended as a replacement. A hybrid is: triangulation capacities
   plan the corridors the lattice A* then searches (survey idea 1).

Features to add independently of the model, since users of TopoR and
of commercial tools expect them: differential pairs and length matching.

## Where we are better already

- **Legality.** KiCad's own checks are the gate, and our verifier is exact.
  TopoR's are neither.
- **KiCad.** Native in and out. TopoR reaches KiCad only through DSN/SES,
  losing its arcs.
- **Speed to a legal board.** Seconds to minutes on the corpus.
- **Placement.** Constraint-driven placement for agents, fab export and
  cost.
